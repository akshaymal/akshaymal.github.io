I asked Claude to build a skill for scaffolding blog posts. The first draft it proposed did what most AI-authoring setups do by default: ask for a title and a topic, then write the whole thing solo, present it as done. That's the exact failure mode a previous post on this site had fallen into — a draft attributed to me with no real back-and-forth. So before any code got written, I asked a lot of underspecified questions instead of accepting the plan: what structure should a post follow, what inputs should ground it, how do we keep it from sounding synthetic. Each answer narrowed the design by one more decision — three post categories with different beat structures, a reusable voice sample, drafting one beat at a time instead of one draft.

## What the skill actually does

The skill runs in nine steps. The ones that matter aren't the ones that write text.

Step 1 doesn't ask for a topic — it asks for a category: technical, hobby-experience, or general. A build log and a story about a marathon shouldn't share a shape. Each category maps to its own three-beat scaffold:

- **Technical** — Context → Mechanism → Implications
- **Hobby-experience** — Hook → Story → Reflection
- **General** — Hook → The Thing → So What

None of those beat names ever become headings in the published post. They're a drafting order, not a template a reader sees.

Before any of that, step 1 also demands a one-sentence thesis — not the topic, the actual point. If I can't state it in one sentence, that's what gets fixed before a word of the post exists.

A few other steps do the real work:

- **Step 2** asks for a writing sample once — three of my own emails, saved to `docs/voice-sample.md` — and reuses it on every post after.
- **Step 4** is where drift usually happens in AI-authored writing, so it's built to make drift expensive: draft a beat, stop, take my edits, then draft the next one. This post is proof — the section above is the Context beat I signed off on before this section existed.
- **Step 6** is a whole-post tightening pass once all three beats are assembled, built to catch the padding that's invisible one beat at a time.
- **Step 7** runs `content-reviewer` against the voice sample instead of a generic tone brief, and **step 8** runs a real `npm run build` — before I ever see a finished draft.

## Why this holds up

The lesson here isn't specific to blog posts. It's that a skill's reliability comes from where it forces a stop, not from how much detail its instructions carry. Nine steps of instructions would have been just as easy to write with the drift built back in — draft solo, tighten later, hope the voice holds. What actually prevents that is three checkpoints placed on purpose: a thesis you have to state before drafting starts, a pause after every beat before the next one begins, and a whole-post tightening pass before anyone outside the process sees it.

That's also why I'm confident this will keep working past this one post. The categories can be wrong, the beat names can change, the voice sample can go stale — those are all easy to fix later. What's load-bearing is the checkpoint structure itself, and that doesn't depend on getting the details right on the first try. A skill that asks you to stop and check is still safe to run again even when the content it produces needs work. A skill that doesn't isn't — no amount of instruction detail fixes that after the fact.
